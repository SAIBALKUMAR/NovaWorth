# Principles & Personas

## 1. Product principles

These are constraints on every feature. If a feature violates one, it does not ship.

### P1. Verified capability is the only source of earned value
No points for logging in, watching without evidence, or clicking "complete." XP and EC require a passed assessment, a reviewed artifact, or a verified real-world outcome.

### P2. Hard is a feature, not a bug
Levels get harder. High levels are rare. We do not inflate pass rates to make people feel good. We do provide scaffolding, retries, and multiple paths to demonstrate the same competence.

### P3. Fairness beats raw IQ
The system rewards demonstrated performance and effort, not identity or inherited advantage. We use validated, skill-relevant measures — never a single global IQ label. Every scoring rule has a fairness review and an adverse-impact check.

### P4. Learner agency is non-negotiable
Recommendations are advice. Any learner can attempt any skill at any level (with a readiness warning), and can switch paths without penalty.

### P5. Effort must be healthy
We reward deliberate practice, not sleep deprivation. There are daily counted-effort caps, wellbeing nudges, and no punitive streak loss. Grinding the same level yields diminishing returns.

### P6. Proof must survive contact with reality
Knowledge tests are necessary but not sufficient. Practical artifacts, simulations, role-play, and real-world impact carry the highest weight at high levels.

### P7. The economy must be honest
Every EC has a funding source. The ledger is double-entry and append-only. Emission is capped by sponsor budget. Prices and rules are public. Clawbacks are possible and appealable.

### P8. Human judgment at the top
AI grades practice and first-pass work. High-stakes decisions (level 90+, credentials, fraud penalties, appeals) include human review.

### P9. Access is a product requirement
WCAG 2.2 AA, captions, transcripts, screen-reader flows, low-bandwidth modes, offline packs, and multiple input methods are planned, not retrofitted.

### P10. No dark patterns
No fake urgency, no pay-to-win, no loot boxes, no infinite scroll in the learning path, no selling attention. Notifications are capped and user-controlled.

### P11. Explain the "why"
Every recommendation, score, rejection, and price shows its top factors. "Because the model said so" is not acceptable.

### P12. Build for a decade
Content is versioned. Credentials are portable. The ledger is auditable. The skill graph can evolve without invalidating past achievement.

## 2. Personas

### 2.1 Maya — the career switcher (primary)
- **Age:** 34
- **Context:** Customer support lead, 9 years experience, two children, 8 hours/week to study.
- **Goal:** Move into a higher-paying, AI-resilient role without a four-year degree.
- **Frustration:** Generic course recommendations; no proof that she can do the job.
- **What she needs:** A high-yield path that uses her communication and operations experience; short wins; mobile-first; clear time estimates; credentials employers recognize.
- **Likely paths:** AI operations, customer success engineering, UX research, data analysis.
- **Success:** Reaches level 30 in one skill in 6 months and redeems a laptop upgrade.

### 2.2 Arjun — the student (primary)
- **Age:** 19
- **Context:** University student, strong at math, loves games, low budget.
- **Goal:** Build a portfolio that beats a degree in the eyes of employers.
- **Frustration:** Lectures are slow; certificates feel worthless.
- **What he needs:** Fast placement, hard challenges, leaderboards, simulators, peer guilds, visible status.
- **Likely paths:** Software engineering, AI agents, game development, quantitative reasoning.
- **Success:** Level 40 in software engineering and a paid bounty before graduation.

### 2.3 Elena — the returner (primary)
- **Age:** 42
- **Context:** Left the workforce for 6 years to care for a parent; anxious about re-entry.
- **Goal:** Rebuild confidence and a credible recent skill record.
- **Frustration:** Gaps in her resume; fear of being judged.
- **What she needs:** Recognition of caregiving and life experience, gentle ramp, private mode, human support, clear "returner" pathways.
- **Likely paths:** Health coordination, elder care, operations, bookkeeping.
- **Success:** Level 20 in care coordination and a verified micro-credential within 90 days.

### 2.4 Kenji — the senior professional (secondary)
- **Age:** 51
- **Context:** Engineering manager, 20 years experience, wants to stay relevant.
- **Goal:** Move from level 60 to 85 in AI systems and mentor others.
- **Frustration:** Beginner content wastes his time; advanced content is shallow.
- **What he needs:** Placement, frontier simulations, expert panels, teaching roles, prestige.
- **Success:** Level 80, recognized as an examiner, earns prestige and mentoring EC.

### 2.5 Priya — the sponsor (economic buyer)
- **Role:** Workforce development lead at a foundation.
- **Goal:** Fund measurable capability growth in an underserved region.
- **Needs:** Budget controls, cohort dashboards, verified outcomes, anti-fraud guarantees, audit exports.
- **Success:** 2,000 learners reach level 20+; cost per verified level is below target; independent audit passes.

### 2.6 Sam — the content author (supply side)
- **Role:** Domain expert and instructional designer.
- **Goal:** Author levels that are rigorous, engaging, and assessable.
- **Needs:** Clear standards, AI drafting tools, review workflow, versioning, attribution, payment per accepted level.
- **Success:** 20 levels published with high learner outcomes and low dispute rates.

### 2.7 Dr. Rao — the examiner (quality gate)
- **Role:** External expert for level 90–100 panels.
- **Goal:** Protect the meaning of world-class.
- **Needs:** Evidence packets, rubrics, conflict-of-interest controls, compensation, time-efficient review.
- **Success:** Panel decisions are consistent, documented, and appeal-resistant.

### 2.8 Leo — the accessibility-first learner (primary)
- **Age:** 27
- **Context:** Blind since birth; screen-reader power user; strong programmer.
- **Goal:** Reach level 50 in software engineering without fighting the interface.
- **Needs:** Semantic HTML, keyboard-only flows, audio descriptions, accessible simulators, captions, no vision-only evidence.
- **Success:** Completes the full loop independently; reports zero blocking accessibility defects.

### 2.9 Nadia — the guardian (secondary)
- **Role:** Parent of a 15-year-old learner.
- **Goal:** Let her child learn and earn safely.
- **Needs:** Guardian consent, spending limits, content controls, privacy, no cash-out, visible progress.
- **Success:** Child reaches level 15 with no safety incidents and no unexpected charges.

### 2.10 Omar — the marketplace partner (supply side)
- **Role:** Local retailer / service provider.
- **Goal:** Fill capacity and reach motivated learners.
- **Needs:** Simple onboarding, guaranteed settlement, fraud protection, demand forecasting, clear catalog rules.
- **Success:** Redemptions grow; settlement is on time; dispute rate is low.

## 4. Anti-personas

We do not optimize for:

- **The credential collector** who wants the fastest certificate with the least learning. The product will feel too hard for them, by design.
- **The bot farm** seeking to farm EC. Integrity controls make this expensive and detectable.
- **The engagement maximizer** who wants infinite content and no endpoint. We want depth, not scroll.
- **The employer** who wants to surveil employees. Learner data belongs to the learner; employer views are consent-based and aggregate by default.
- **The speculator** who wants to trade or cash out EC. EC is a restricted learning credit in v1.

## 5. Design implications

1. **Every screen must answer "what do I do next and why?"**
2. **The next level must always show difficulty, time, reward, and proof type.**
3. **Failure must be informative, not shameful.** Show what was missing and the shortest path to retry.
4. **The wallet must show provenance.** Every EC balance links to the award that created it.
5. **The catalog must be browsable without onboarding.** Agency over funnel.
6. **Spatial experiences must have a 2D equivalent.** No skill is gated by hardware.
7. **The first 10 minutes must produce a real signal**, not a survey slog.
