# Vision

## 1. The world we are designing for

AI systems increasingly produce the goods, and AI robotic flows increasingly deliver the services. In that world, the old bargain — "do a job, earn a living" — weakens. Material scarcity does not disappear, but it stops being the main constraint on human survival. The new constraint is **capability, judgment, trust, and the ability to keep adapting**.

Education Economy is built on a blunt thesis:

> In an economy of AI abundance, the only durable differentiator for a human is the ability to learn, prove, and apply something real — continuously.

That is the **Learning Race**. It is not a race against other people in a zero-sum sense; it is a race against the pace of change. The product makes that race visible, fair, rewarding, and — importantly — playable for a lifetime.

## 2. The problem

Today's learning products fail the people who need them most:

1. **Credentials are weak signals.** A certificate often proves attendance, not capability.
2. **Content is disconnected from consequence.** Learners finish a course and have nothing to show, earn, or apply.
3. **Difficulty is flat.** Most platforms reward streaks and completion, not the painful, high-value climb to mastery.
4. **Advice is generic.** "Learn to code" ignores a person's existing experience, constraints, and comparative advantage.
5. **Assessment is easy to game.** Multiple-choice quizzes and AI-generated answers make proof cheap.
6. **Learning is lonely and unsustainable.** Motivation collapses without status, community, and material payoff.
7. **The top end is missing.** Nothing takes a competent person from level 40 to genuinely world-class level 100.

## 3. The insight

People will invest enormous effort when four things are true:

1. The challenge is **calibrated** — hard enough to matter, not so hard it feels arbitrary.
2. Progress is **visible and scarce** — levels mean something, and high levels are rare.
3. Effort converts into **real advantage** — status, access, tools, services, and material rewards.
4. The learner believes the system is **fair** — the rules are transparent and cheating is punished.

Education Economy is the machine that makes all four true at once.

### 4.2 The 100-level ladder

Every skill has 100 levels across 10 bands:

| Band | Levels | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–10 | Foundation: survive and operate |
| 2 | 11–20 | Literacy: confident, safe use |
| 3 | 21–30 | Practitioner: repeatable quality |
| 4 | 31–40 | Builder: make real things |
| 5 | 41–50 | Specialist: depth and diagnosis |
| 6 | 51–60 | Professional: deliver under constraints |
| 7 | 61–70 | Senior: systems and scale |
| 8 | 71–80 | Architect: design under uncertainty |
| 9 | 81–90 | Master: lead, teach, handle crises |
| 10 | 91–100 | World-class: original contribution, verified impact |

Level 100 is not "completed the syllabus." It requires an artifact, verified real-world impact, peer review, and an expert panel. After 100, the ladder continues as **prestige**: frontier simulations, combined-skill missions, research, mentoring, and bounties — with deliberately diminishing raw pay and rising status.

### 4.3 The economy

- **XP** measures mastery. It is permanent, non-transferable, and never spent.
- **Education Credits (EC)** are spendable. They are earned through verified learning and funded by sponsors, employers, governments, and marketplace margin.
- **Reputation** measures trust. It unlocks higher-stakes opportunities and moderation power.
- **Prestige** is post-100 status. It is not bought and not transferred.

The economy is not a free-money scheme. It is a **sponsored learning dividend** with strict emission controls, a double-entry ledger, and anti-fraud operations. Phase 5 explores livelihood-scale payouts; early phases are explicit that EC is supplemental.

### 4.4 The unfair-advantage matcher

Most people do not know which skill will give them the most return for the least wasted effort. Onboarding builds an **experience graph** from work history, life experience (travel, sports, caregiving, hobbies, languages, home projects), interests, constraints, and a short adaptive diagnostic. The recommender returns:

- 3–4 **high-yield paths** where aptitude, interest, transferable experience, and demand intersect,
- one **quick win** to build momentum,
- one **stretch path** for long-term upside,
- and an always-available full catalog.

The learner always has agency. The system advises; it does not gatekeep.

### 4.5 Modalities

Learning happens in whatever format fits the skill and the moment:

- text + Socratic AI tutor,
- interactive video with checkpoints,
- adaptive quizzes and retrieval practice,
- AI role-play with rubric scoring,
- deterministic simulators and live-fire incidents,
- projects, portfolios, and real-world bounties.

The platform is available on web, mobile, and Apple Vision Pro, with an optional metaverse/WebXR layer where spatial presence adds real value.

## 5. What success looks like

### For the learner
"I know exactly what I am getting better at, I can prove it, and it changes what I can do and buy."

### For society
A visible, trusted map of human capability that helps people move into the skills that matter, without leaving behind the people who start with less.

### For sponsors
A measurable way to fund capability growth in specific regions, industries, and populations — with auditable outcomes.

### For the product
A durable economy where verified learning supply meets sponsored demand, fraud stays below 1%, and learners keep climbing because the next level is genuinely worth it.

## 6. Non-goals (v1)

- A universal basic income or a cash-equivalent currency.
- A replacement for accredited education, licensure, or professional regulation.
- A pure IQ test or a single "human worth" score.
- A fully autonomous AI teacher with no human review at high stakes.
- A metaverse-first product. Spatial is an enhancement, not the core loop.
- A marketplace for anything and everything. The catalog is curated and restricted.

## 7. Why now

1. **AI makes content cheap; proof becomes expensive.** The scarce thing is credible evidence of human capability.
2. **AI makes cheating cheap.** Verification, proctoring, artifacts, and human panels become differentiators.
3. **AI makes personalization cheap.** Adaptive tutoring and simulation can operate at a scale that was impossible five years ago.
4. **Spatial computing is emerging.** Vision Pro and WebXR make embodied practice viable for physical and interpersonal skills.
5. **Institutions need a new signal.** Employers, governments, and funders are looking for verifiable, granular capability data.

## 8. The bet

If we can make the 100-level climb **fair, hard, visible, and materially meaningful**, then a large segment of humanity will choose to keep learning — not because they are told to, but because the economy rewards it and the status is real.

That is the product.
